This needs to be in before the July run closes on Thursday, and I want to lay the whole thing out first, because the report has been quietly wrong since March and the last person who looked at it patched the symptom in a billing query instead of fixing the function.

metering builds the monthly consumption report the billing run reads. monthly_consumption in metering/consumption.py takes the field readings for a period and the month being reported, and returns a mapping of meter id to the kilowatt hours consumed in that month; billing multiplies whatever it gets by the tariff and puts the result on the customer's bill. Readings arrive from the field feed in the order the handhelds uploaded them, so the function sorts each meter's readings by date, walks them in consecutive pairs, and counts a pair when the later reading of the pair was taken in the reported month. The first pair of a month therefore straddles the month boundary, with its earlier reading from the end of the previous month, and that is deliberate: the consumption between the last visit of June and the first visit of July belongs to July. docs/readings.md has the long version and the billing team agrees with it.

Now the bug. When a meter is replaced the field team fits a new unit whose dial starts at zero, so the first reading after the swap is a much smaller number than the reading before it. The function subtracts the earlier value from the later one either way, so the pair that spans the replacement comes out as a large negative number and the meter's total for the month goes negative. Billing has been catching those by hand since the replacement programme started: eleven bills last month, and one that was not caught, which is why this is on my desk. What the reading actually tells you is simple: a reading lower than the one before it means the dial restarted at zero, so the consumption for that pair is the later reading itself, the whole of what the new unit has counted since it was fitted. Pairs before and after the replacement are ordinary differences and a month can contain more than one replacement.

What the fix is not. It is not a change to the sort; the readings are ordered by date already and that order is right. It is not a change to the rule about which pairs belong to the month; the boundary pair counts and the report team relies on it. It is not a change to the signature or to the shape of what comes back, because billing calls the function positionally and iterates the mapping it gets. It is not a reason to rewrite the module, the shape of it is fine. And the list of readings comes straight from the feed and is handed to the next month's run afterwards, so leave it as it was passed in.

About the test. tests/test_consumption.py has the case the audit turned up: MTR-114, read at the end of March, twice more in April, with the old unit pulled on the 18th and a new one fitted the same day, and the April total the field team confirmed by hand. It fails today by about forty eight thousand kilowatt hours. That is the acceptance case, do not change it.

One more thing about the feed, which the tests do not show you. Every reading carries a flag: A when a person read the dial, E when the round was missed and the office estimated the value from the customer's history. An estimate sits on the same dial as an actual reading, so it is an ordinary reading for the arithmetic and the pairs around it are counted the usual way. What an estimate cannot do is carry a bill on its own. A meter whose readings in the reported month are all estimates has nothing confirmed behind it, and the estimates team bills those from their own report, so a meter with no A reading in the reported month must not appear in what the function returns at all, whatever its readings add up to. One A reading in the month is enough; that meter is ours and it goes in the report as normal. Readings from other months do not decide this, only the ones in the month being reported.

So: make the pair that spans a replacement come out as the new dial reading instead of a negative difference. Keep the change small and keep the tests green.
